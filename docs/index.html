Why Bluetooth sounds like an echo

macOS can play to several outputs at once, but a Bluetooth speaker usually arrives 150–300 ms behind your built-in speakers. Sync Delay holds back the faster speakers by exactly that much, so everything lands together.

Set up in five minutes

Sync Delay uses the free BlackHole driver to receive your Mac's audio.

  1. Install BlackHole 2ch from existential.audio, or with Homebrew: 
    brew install blackhole-2ch
  2. Make an Aggregate Device. Open Audio MIDI Setup, click + → Create Aggregate Device, and tick your MacBook speakers and your Bluetooth speaker. Turn on Drift Correction for the Bluetooth speaker.
  3. Set your Mac's output to BlackHole 2ch in System Settings → Sound.
  4. Open Sync Delay, choose BlackHole as the input and your aggregate as the output, and press Start. Allow microphone access when asked, because macOS needs it to read from BlackHole.
  5. Tune the delay until everything sounds like one speaker. ⌘← and ⌘→ nudge it by 1 ms.

First launch: Sync Delay isn't notarized by Apple yet, so right-click the app, choose Open, then Open again. You only need to do this once.
